Legend: what every colour, mark and box means

Blue: measured by the band during the night. Sleep onset is estimated from the band's recorded sleep stages.
Orange: measured by the band during the following day.
Green: your own answers from the check-ins. A filled dot is a reported time or rating; a ring is your reported bedtime.
Light → dark blue: in bed but not yet asleep, asleep, awake in the night.
Dark → light green: your sleep-quality rating, 5 → 1.
Grey line or bar: the difference between reported and measured. It is not a verdict.
Light / dark green dot: your energy at midday / in the evening.
Centre line: the day sits above it (productive hours, steps or active minutes), the night before below.
Ring on the heart-rate line: shown only when an exact peak timestamp is available.
Grey hatching / "no data": nothing recorded. A gap is shown as empty and never estimated.
Dashed line: an average for the week, or (in the scatter) where reported equals measured.
Blue-edged box: about the night.
Orange-edged box: about the following day.
Green-edged box: your answers, or the comparison with them.
Grey-edged box: the night and the day together.

About these comparisons

Dates and times use Europe/Amsterdam. Each night is paired with its wake day. Sleep charts use the main recorded session (or the longest session if no main session is marked); other sessions are not added to that night. Night heart rate covers 22:00–06:00 and day heart rate 06:00–22:00. The daily heart-rate line shows one-minute observed averages, with missing minutes left blank. Markers show reported times and Fitbit estimated onset; no independent heart-rate sleep-onset algorithm is applied. Peak values are observed readings; their exact times are unavailable. Early-morning reported clock times use the study date as confirmed by the participant; other reported times retain their supplied dates; ambiguous or inconsistent durations stay empty. Active minutes and reported awakenings were not collected. Check-in completeness assumes one midday and one evening response per study date. These comparisons describe associations, not causes or medical advice.
